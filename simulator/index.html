Calculator · three free tools, no sign-up

Solar panels in Luxembourg: are they worth it for your home?

Tool 1: how much your roof would produce and earn each year. Tool 2: how many years until the system pays for itself, with the 2026 Klimabonus or with the 15-year guaranteed feed-in tariff, and which of the two suits you. Tool 3: what a heat pump costs and saves in place of your current heating, with or without these panels. Everything is computed in your browser from official sources: yield from PVGIS, subsidies from guichet.lu, ILR grid tariffs, the grand-ducal regulation on feed-in tariffs. Benchmark: 38,187 installations are already connected in Luxembourg (2 November 2026).

Frequently asked questionsWhat people in Luxembourg ask before installing

How much does 1 kWp of solar panels produce in Luxembourg?

About 1,070 kWh a year for a due-south roof pitched at 35° in Luxembourg City (PVGIS, 2005-2023 average, 14% system losses), 1,010 kWh for a south-east or south-west roof, 860 kWh for an east or west roof. The north of the country (Clervaux, Wiltz) produces 3% less, the south (Esch, Capellen) 2% more. A 6 kWp system therefore produces 5,500 to 6,400 kWh a year, 1.4 to 1.6 times the consumption of an average household (3,900 kWh).

How much is the Klimabonus for solar PV in 2026?

For a self-consumption system of power P (in kWp, at least 2), mounted on the roof or façade or integrated into the building envelope, the subsidy is P × (1,155 − 33 × P) euros below 15 kWp and 10,000 euros from 15 kWp: €3,168 for 3 kWp, €5,742 for 6 kWp, €8,250 for 10 kWp. A battery of capacity Q (kWh) adds Q × (500 − 500/18 × Q) euros, €2,250 from 9 kWh. The subsidy can be deducted from the invoice by an installer listed in the register of intermediary applicants (pre-financing, optional) or claimed afterwards through the standard procedure. Eligible invoices: 4 January 2026 to 31 December 2029, or up to 31 December 2031 if the system was ordered by 31 December 2029 at the latest. Condition: waive any guaranteed remuneration for the electricity fed in. Since 1 October 2026 (invoice date), an energy management system (HEMS) that controls at least two devices (panels, battery, heat pump, charging station, controllable immersion heater) also receives €500.

Klimabonus or guaranteed feed-in tariff: which to choose?

The two cannot be combined. The Klimabonus cuts the upfront investment and suits small or medium systems that self-consume a good share of their output. The guaranteed tariff (13.3 c€/kWh for a first feed-in in 2026, 12.9 c€ in 2027, for 15 years, up to 10 kW) pays better for the surplus and becomes competitive when the system is large relative to consumption. Tool 2 computes both for your case; as a rule, the more you feed in, the closer option B gets to option A. For an offer signed between 1 October 2024 and 4 March 2026, a transitional scheme lets you combine the guaranteed tariff with a 20% subsidy capped at €500/kWp, provided the final invoice is dated 31 December 2026 at the latest (Klima-Agence, 17 December 2025); this scheme is not simulated here.

How much does a solar PV system cost in Luxembourg?

2026 quotes seen from installers range from €1,600 to €2,200 per kWp installed for 6 to 15 kWp, i.e. €9,600 to €13,200 for 6 kWp, VAT included. Supply and installation on a dwelling qualifies for the super-reduced VAT rate of 3%. After the Klimabonus, a 6 kWp system costs about €3,900 to €7,500.

What is a self-consumed kWh worth?

Only the variable components of the bill are avoided: energy (about 13 c€/kWh in 2026), the grid's volumetric charge (5.10 c€), the compensation mechanism (−3.71 c€ from August to December 2026 thanks to temporary State aid, −0.10 c€ from January to July), the electricity tax (0.10 c€), all subject to 8% VAT: about 16 c€/kWh from August to December 2026, and close to 20 c€ at the January to July 2026 tariff, which still includes a State contribution; the 2027 tariff is not published as of 2 November 2026. Fixed charges (metering, reference power) remain due. Counting 25 c€ because "my bill divided by my kWh gives 25 cents" overstates the gain.

Do you need a battery?

A battery lifts the self-consumed share from about 30% to 50-60% for a typical house, but costs €700 to €1,100 per kWh installed (an indicative range assumed by the tool, with no published source; €900 by default), part of which the Klimabonus refunds (up to €2,250). With a surplus sold at 5 c€ and an avoided kWh worth 20 c€, each stored kWh earns 15 c€: a 7 kWh battery cycled 250 times a year earns about €260 a year for €2,800 to €5,600 of net investment after the Klimabonus. It therefore usually lengthens the payback time; tool 2 shows it for your case.

Is the income from a solar PV system taxable?

For a private individual operating a system of 1 to 30 kWp, the direct tax administration accepts, as a matter of tolerance, that it is a hobby activity not counted in taxable income (circular L.I.R. no. 14/2 of 5 June 2023, summarised by Klima-Agence). With pure self-consumption there is no commercial activity at all.

How do I check my roof's potential?

The national solar cadastre (energie.geoportail.lu) rates every roof in the country by its potential; Klima-Agence offers free, neutral advice. For a fine estimate, PVGIS accepts the exact address, shading and actual orientation.

How much is the Klimabonus for a heat pump in 2026?

For an air-to-water heat pump in an existing single-family house, the state pays €10,000 (€12,000 for an order placed between 1 January 2026 and 30 June 2027 and invoiced by 31 December 2028 at the latest) when it replaces a gas or oil boiler or direct electric heating, and otherwise €6,000 (€8,000 for an order placed in the same period). On top of that come €1,500 for removing the tank when an oil boiler is replaced, €2,000 if the house is in a water protection zone, and 50% of the cost of upgrading the heat distribution, up to €2,000. Technical conditions: a COP of at least 3.1 (air at 7 °C, water at 35 °C) and, for a heat pump without speed or output modulation, a buffer tank of at least 30 L per kW of thermal output (guichet.lu, page updated on 11 August 2026, accessed 2 November 2026). Some municipalities add their own grant; tool 3 does not count it.

Is a heat pump worth it in Luxembourg?

It depends above all on your current consumption and the price of the energy replaced. Tool 3 defaults: heating oil at €1.601/L (official maximum price in force since 27 October 2026), gas at 9.5 c€/kWh (tool assumption; ILR: 9.14 c€/kWh on average for a household in 2025), electricity bought at €0.20 per kWh and solar surplus sold at the tool 2 price. An air-to-water heat pump delivers several kWh of heat per kWh of electricity, all the more when the heating water is cooler (underfloor heating) and the winter mild: the tool computes this hour by hour with the formula published by Ruhnau et al. (2019). Panels that power the heat pump during the day cut the bill further. The Klimabonus and the quoted price weigh as much as the energy: tool 3 does the calculation for your house.

Do you need a buffer tank with a heat pump?

The Klimabonus only requires one for a heat pump without speed or output modulation: at least 30 L per kW of thermal output (guichet.lu). With panels, a tank heated at midday with the solar surplus raises the self-consumed share by a few points, but each kWh kept this way only earns the gap between the purchase price of electricity and the sale price of the surplus. In the tool's default case (300 L, €1,800 installed, tool assumptions), the tank does not pay for itself on its own; tool 3 shows what it brings in your case.

Method and sources

  
This calculator is a decision aid, not a quote: it knows neither your roof's shading, nor your hourly consumption profile, nor your contract. It sells nothing and sends no data: everything is computed in your browser. The assumptions are displayed, adjustable and dated; please report any regulatory change to us.

  
Calculation. Production = power × PVGIS yield (orientation, pitch) × canton factor, spread by month along the PVGIS profile for Luxembourg City. Self-consumed share without battery = 30% × (production ÷ consumption)−0.6, bounded between 10 and 90%; a battery of Q kWh adds 35% × (1 − e−Q ÷ (0.5 × daily consumption)). Annual gain = self-consumed kWh × value of the avoided kWh (indexed by the chosen rise) + kWh fed in × surplus price (option A) or guaranteed tariff for 15 years then surplus price (option B). Production declines at the chosen ageing rate; the inverter is replaced in year 13. Payback time = first year in which the cumulative cash flow turns positive (interpolated); net gain = cumulative cash flow at 25 years in nominal euros, undiscounted by default (with a discount rate: sum of the discounted flows; payback time and figure 2 remain undiscounted); IRR = rate that sets the present value of the flows to zero. No maintenance or insurance cost is counted. The self-consumption formula is a heuristic specific to this calculator, set to the order of magnitude of 30% without a battery, with no published reference. The battery is neither replaced nor degraded over 25 years: with a battery, the result is optimistic. The optional HEMS is counted as a cost and, in option A only, with its subsidy; its effect on the self-consumed share is not modelled.

  
Tool 3 calculation (heating). Tool assumptions unless stated otherwise. Useful heat demand = consumption × efficiency of the current system (gas: 10.6 kWh per billed m³; heating oil: 10 kWh per litre), or floor area × demand per m² by construction period; hot water: 800 kWh per person per year, at most 40% of the total. Space heating is spread hour by hour in proportion to the degree-hours below 15 °C of the typical weather year, hot water and other uses along fixed daily profiles. Hourly heat pump COP = 0.85 × (6.08 − 0.09 ΔT + 0.0005 ΔT²), at least 1, where ΔT is the gap between the flow temperature and the outdoor temperature T, taken as at least 10 °C (threshold: tool assumption, Ruhnau et al. use 15 °C; formula of Ruhnau et al., 2019, correction factor included); flow at 40 − T °C (radiators) or 30 − 0.5 × T °C (underfloor heating), hot water at 50 °C; a direct electric back-up covers whatever the available output does not. The buffer tank is heated 15 °C above the flow temperature with the solar surplus (1.163 Wh per litre per °C, losses of 1% per hour) and releases its heat when the panels no longer suffice. Battery: 90% round-trip efficiency, 0.5 kW per kWh, 5 kW at most. Bill = fuel + electricity bought − surplus sold + maintenance (boiler €200/year, heat pump €150/year); fuel rises at the chosen rate, electricity as in tool 2; with panels, the inverter is replaced in year 13. Payback and net gain = spending difference with the current heating over 20 years. Limits: a single weather year, no panel ageing or battery replacement in this tool, single-family houses only, cancelled gas standing charge not counted, municipal grants not counted.
